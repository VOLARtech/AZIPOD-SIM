# Azipod Bridge

**Learn to handle ships, from a small harbour tug to a giant container ship, right in your web browser.**

👉 **Open it:** https://volartech.github.io/AZIPOD-SIM/

It's free, runs on phones, tablets and computers, and once installed it keeps working with no internet connection.

> **For training and fun only. Not for real navigation.** The ships behave very much like real ones, but never exactly the same. Don't use it to plan a real manoeuvre.

## What is it?

You stand on the bridge of a full-size ship and bring her into port: through a narrow channel, around a tight turn, and gently alongside the quay. The ship drifts with the wind and the current, slows down in shallow water, and doesn't stop just because you let go of the controls, just like the real thing.

It started as a trainer for cruise ships with *Azipods* (propellers in pods under the ship that turn in any direction). It now has 22 ships, each with the controls its real crew would use.

## What you can do

- **Steer 22 different ships**: cruise ships, ferries, tankers, container ships, tugs, an icebreaker and more (see the list below).
- **Sail in real places.** PortMiami, built from the official sea chart, with around 13,000 real buildings, the causeway bridges and the container cranes. The passenger terminal in Amsterdam. Practice harbours, a river, an oil terminal, an ice field and the open sea.
- **Look out of the window in 3D.** See the view from the wheelhouse or either bridge wing, follow the ship from behind, or go underwater and watch the propellers turn. Day, dusk and night, with ship lights and lit-up windows after dark.
- **See where you're heading.** A forecast line on the water shows where your ship will be in the next few minutes, so you can correct early.
- **Handle real situations.** Tie up with mooring lines, call tugs to push and pull, cope with gusty wind and strong current, pass another ship in a narrow channel, break a path through ice, escort a loaded tanker, or hold a supply ship perfectly still next to an oil platform.
- **Hear and feel it.** Engines and propellers that sound the way they're working, the bow thruster roar, fenders squeaking against the quay, mooring lines creaking. On Android phones you also feel the controls click and the bumps.
- **Run sea trials.** Measure turning circles and stopping distances, the way shipyards test new ships.

## The ships

| Kind | Ships | How you drive them |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Cruise and training ships | Two large Azipod cruise ships (293 m and 278 m), a 135 m Azipod training ship, a classic twin-propeller cruise ship | Pod levers and a joystick, or propeller levers |
| Ferries | A car and passenger ferry, a 70 m fast catamaran ferry (36 knots) | Propeller levers; water-jet controls on the fast ferry |
| Cargo ships | An oil supertanker (loaded or empty), a gas carrier, a medium and a huge 24,000-container ship, a bulk carrier (also a version with tall spinning *rotor sails*) | Engine telegraph and steering wheel, like a real cargo ship |
| Tugs | Three harbour tugs of different designs | Tug controls, with a towline you can pay out and release |
| Offshore | A platform supply ship, an anchor-handling tug | *Dynamic positioning*: the computer holds position while you set where to go |
| Special | An icebreaker, a river push boat with four barges, a navy frigate, a 90 m superyacht | Each with its own controls |

Every ship (except the barge convoy) is shown with a detailed 3D model, so it looks like the kind of ship it is.

## Where you can sail

Fourteen scenarios, from easy to tricky:

- **Practice harbours**: a training quay, a dredged channel where the banks pull at the ship, and a swing basin to turn around in.
- **Real ports**: PortMiami's Terminal V and the passenger terminal in Amsterdam.
- **Cargo work**: berthing at an oil jetty, escorting a loaded supertanker with a tug.
- **Traffic**: meeting and overtaking another ship in a channel.
- **River**: taking a barge convoy down a river bend and under a bridge.
- **Fast ferry**: entering a harbour at speed.
- **Offshore**: a supply run to an oil platform, holding position.
- **Ice**: breaking a channel with the icebreaker.
- **Ocean**: a passage under rotor sails, and open water for sea trials.

If a ship is too big or too deep for a harbour, the simulator tells you and picks one that fits.

## Controls on a keyboard

On a phone or tablet, just use the levers and wheels on screen. On a computer you can also use the keyboard:

- **Azipod ships:** `A` `D` turn the left pod, `W` `S` set its power; `J` `L` and `I` `K` do the same for the right pod; `Q` `E` push the bow sideways; arrow keys use the joystick.
- **Cargo ships and lever ships:** `W` `S` faster or slower, `A` `D` steer, `X` stop the engine, `M` centre the rudder.
- **Everywhere:** `V` switches between chart, split screen and 3D; `C` changes the 3D camera; `H` turns the chart with the ship.

## What's new

- **20 more ships.** It began with three Azipod ships; now there are 22, covering almost every way a ship can be driven: engines with a telegraph, adjustable propellers, tug drives, water jets, computer-held positioning and more.
- **Realistic 3D ships.** Detailed 3D models replace the simple box shapes, fitted to each ship's real size.
- **New places and challenges.** River, ice, offshore platform, oil terminal, tanker escort, fast ferry and ocean scenarios, plus other ships to meet and overtake.
- **Tugs come when you call them.** Harbour tugs wait out of sight until you ask for help, then leave again when you let them go.
- **Smoother and more reliable.** Switching ships is quicker, the chart draws faster, and the app copes better with slow or missing internet.
- **Fixes.** Ships now face the right way, with their propellers at the stern, and each bridge camera sits where the real wheelhouse is.

```bash
npm ci
npm run dev        # run it locally
npm test           # check the physics still behaves
npm run build:all  # build the app and the offline copy
```

Every change to `main` is tested and published automatically. The physics, data sources, ship-model tools and every command are described in [docs/TECHNICAL.md](docs/TECHNICAL.md); the plan behind the fleet expansion is in [docs/expansion/PLAN.md](docs/expansion/PLAN.md).
